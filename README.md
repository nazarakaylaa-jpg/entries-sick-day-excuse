One line under 200 characters that gets you out of work and gets a laugh. The entry is written by `main.py`, which prints a single sick-day excuse to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The excuse

> Can't come in — I slipped on the dog's toy, landed on the remote and accidentally ordered 42 garden gnomes. I have to stay home to refuse the delivery.

The idea: a small household accident escalates into something too ridiculous to invent on the spot, and the reason to stay home is a real one — somebody has to be there to turn the delivery away. Absurd, but just plausible enough that a boss might half believe it and a coworker would laugh. The twist lands at the end, in the second sentence: the gnomes are the joke, staying home to refuse them is the excuse.

Why this shape, against the judge's brief: no fever, no dog ate my homework, no dead grandmother — a fresh mishap instead. It reads like a real message typed to a boss, not a joke built for a contest: one breath, two sentences, every word doing work.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

The output is one line, 151 characters, UTF-8, no trailing newline.
